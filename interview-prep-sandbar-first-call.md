**Best guess:** a small Atlantic Canadian company selling online booking and scheduling software to small appointment-based businesses (clinics, salons, tour and rental operators, trades). It exhibits at regional small-business or industry trade shows. Booth visitors are business owners or managers. The goal of a follow-up is probably to book a demo or start a trial through Sandbar's booking page.

**Other possibilities to rule out:**
- Staff or shift scheduling (rostering) software sold to employers, not customer bookings
- Software for one industry only (for example only health clinics or only tourism)
- A scheduling *service* (people doing the booking), not software

**Why it matters for the build:**
- What they sell and their price set what "hot" means and what the right next step is.
- Deal size and sales cycle tell you whether 30 days is enough to see a sale, or only a demo.
- Whether booth visitors are mainly prospects, existing customers or partners decides who should *not* get a draft.

The questions in **3.0 Company and customers** confirm or correct this in the first few minutes. **If the answer contradicts the guess, drop the guess straight away** and adjust the "Definitions" and "Next step" questions to their words.

## 1. What to know before you start

**What the grading rules give away** (from `Sprint_3_general.md`):
- **"15 real conversations"**: the booth files probably hold more than 15 people (badge scans, people who only took swag, vendors, competitors, students). You need to learn how *they* tell a real conversation apart.
- **"Appears once"**: expect duplicates. The same person may show up as a badge scan, a business card and a note on Owen's phone, with different spellings or emails.
- **"No one who shouldn't be emailed gets a draft"**: some people must be left out, for example existing customers, competitors, people who said "don't contact me", people who already booked or opted out of the scan. Find out who they are.
- **"Mentions something specific they said at the booth"**: there are conversation notes somewhere, so find out where they live and what format they're in.
- **"Personal or family details never go in a draft"**: the notes have personal details mixed in (kids, illness, vacations). Ask what kind, and why it's in there.
- **"Priority" and "next step" are for you to find out**: these are *their* categories. Get the actual labels and the rules behind them, with examples.
- **"What Nadia wants to know 30 days after a show"**: this is the real success measure, so ask her directly.

**Likely story** (a hypothesis, so don't lead with it). Owen talks to people at the booth and jots notes. A badge-scan list arrives later from the show organizer. Erin cleans and imports it. Follow-up is late, generic, or never happens, so leads go cold and nobody can trace a sale back to a show. The first root cause you hear will probably be "the leads are bad" or "follow-up is slow". Don't take it. Ask *why* it's slow and *what's missing* when someone sits down to write the email (probably the context from the conversation).

**Group dynamics:**
- Nadia is the boss and paying the bill. Owen may feel the "leads go cold" story is aimed at him, the way Priya felt in Sprint 1. Erin probably sees the data mess nobody else sees.
- Address questions **by name** so all three answer. If one person answers for another, follow up with the other: "Erin, is that how it looks from your side?"
- **Disagreements are signal.** Note them and stay neutral. Don't take sides or decide anything on the call.
- Keep each message short: one or two questions. Long multi-part messages get partial answers and burn the clock.

**Time budget (30 min):**

| Min | Block | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Open, roles and the company in their words | What Sandbar sells, to whom, at what price; who does what around a show |
| 3–10 | The last show, walked through | Real sequence, real tools, where it breaks |

## 3. Question bank (open first, then probe)

### 3.0 Company and customers (2–3 min, before anything about shows)
Ask Nadia first, then let Owen add to it.
- "Before we get into the shows, can you tell me in a couple of sentences what Sandbar does, and who your customers are?"
  - Probe: "What kind of business is a typical customer? How big are they?"
  - Probe: "Who at that business decides to buy, the owner or a manager?"
- "What does a new customer pay you, roughly, per month or per year?" (needed to judge the $9,000)
- "From the first conversation to a signed customer, how long does it usually take? What are the steps in between?" (tells you what a realistic 30-day outcome is)
- "Which shows do you go to, and who walks past the booth?" (business owners, students, existing customers, competitors, vendors)
- "Where do most of your new customers come from today, apart from shows?" (gives a baseline: are shows a big source or a small one?)
- "Owen, when a booth conversation goes *well*, what happens next? What do you want them to do?" (links to "next step" and the booking page)

### A. The last show, walked through (Priya's "tell me about yesterday")
- "Tell me about the most recent show. Owen, take me through a typical conversation at the booth, from hello to goodbye."
  - Probe: "What did you write down, and where? Can you show me what that looks like?" (scanner app, cards, phone notes, notebook, voice memos)
  - Probe: "Did anyone else work the booth? Did they take notes the same way?"
- "Erin, what lands on your desk after the show, and when?"
  - Probe: "What do you do with it? What does it look like on your screen?"
  - Probe: "How many names did the last show produce? How many of those were real conversations?"
- "Then what happens? Who writes the follow-up, when, and what does it say?"
  - Probe: "How long after the show did the first email go out last time?"
  - Probe: "Can you show me one you actually sent?"

### B. One specific failure and one specific win (Priya's Thanksgiving story, Tanya's "last Thursday")
- "Is there one person from a recent show you're sure *should* have turned into something but didn't? What happened, step by step?"
  - Probe the timeline: when they talked, what was said, when the follow-up went out, what it said, what came back.
- "Is there one that *did* work? What was different?"

### C. The $9,000 and what Nadia wants to know
- "Nadia, what makes up the $9,000?" (booth, travel, staff time, swag) "Is that the full cost, or is Owen's time on top?"
- "What has a show brought in that you *do* know about? How did you find out?"
- **"Thirty days after a show, what do you want to be able to look at, and what question should it answer?"** (Ask Nadia directly. Probe for specifics: a number, a list, per-person status, booked demos, revenue.)
- "If you had that, what decision would you make with it?" (keep, drop or change shows; change how the booth is staffed)
- "Owen and Erin, is that the same thing you'd want to know?"

### D. Definitions (what grading depends on)
- "When you look back at a show list, how do you tell a real conversation from someone who just got scanned?"
- **Priority:** "When you go through the list, how do you decide who's hot and who isn't? What words do you use?" Then: "Give me an example of each."
  - Probe: "Who decides? Do Owen and Nadia ever disagree on one?"
- **Next step:** "For a hot one, what's the right next move: a demo, a call, a quote, sending something?" "What about a lukewarm one?" "Is 'do nothing' ever right?"
- **Never email:** "Is there anyone on a show list who should *never* get a follow-up from you?" (Probe for existing customers, competitors, people who asked not to be contacted, vendors, people already booked, partners.)
- **Duplicates:** "Erin, does the same person ever show up more than once? How do you spot it?"
- **Personal details:** "What kind of personal things end up in the booth notes?" "Why do you write them down?" (rapport and memory) "Where's the line on what goes in an email?"

### E. Root cause: don't take the first answer
- "In your own words, why do these conversations go cold?" Ask all three. Expect three different answers.
- Second why: "Why does that happen?" or "What would have had to be true for that follow-up to go out on day two?"
- Counterfactual (Tanya's "if the list were perfect at 8am"): "If Owen had a perfect list the morning after the show, what would still get in the way?"
- "When Owen sits down to write the follow-up, what's missing that he wishes he had?"
- Look for the *inverse* cases (Priya's polite cruise emails): "Was anyone who looked like a tire-kicker actually a serious buyer, or the other way round?"

### F. What they've tried, and their constraints
- "What have you tried before to fix this?" (show organizer's lead app, CRM, templates, a VA) "Why did it stop?"
- "What would you definitely *not* want?" (auto-sending, generic blasts, another login, sending personal details)
- "Who would approve a draft before it goes out? Who would it be sent from?"
- "Where does contact information go now, and where should it not go?" (privacy, consent from badge scans, CASL in Canada)
- "Who would look after this once I'm gone? What would you be comfortable paying a month to run it?" (Done-means item 4)
- "What tools do you already live in? (CRM, booking page, email)" "Who has admin access?"

### G. Close
- "Is there anything I haven't asked about that you think matters?"
- Data asks (below), then: "I'd like 15 minutes one-on-one with two of you later this week. Is that okay?"

## 4. Data to ask for (files come only when you ask)
Ask in the last five minutes, specifically:
1. **The raw files from the most recent show**: badge-scan export, Owen's notes (photos of cards, phone notes, notebook pages) and anything other booth staff wrote.
2. **The same files for an older show whose results are known**, *plus* someone marking each person as real conversation yes/no, the right priority, the right next step, and "never email" yes/no. This is your ground truth for the 12-of-15 target, like Tanya marking the openings in Sprint 2.
3. **The current customer list and any do-not-contact list**, to catch the people who shouldn't be emailed.
4. **Two or three follow-up emails Owen actually sent**, one that got a reply and one that didn't. These show tone and format for the drafts.
5. **What happened afterwards**: demo bookings or deals traced back to a show, even if rough.
6. **The cost breakdown of the $9,000**, if Nadia has it.
7. *(Optional)* **A one-page description or price sheet** of what Sandbar sells, if one exists.

## 6. Techniques from the reference interviews
- Start from a **concrete recent instance**, not the general process ("yesterday" or "the last show").
- Ask **"what does that look like on your screen?"** to get past summaries.
- **Reconcile numbers out loud** ("Earlier you said X, now Y. Which is closer?").
- Ask for **one specific failure with a timeline**, then probe each delay.
- **Define their words** ("What does urgent mean to you?" becomes "What does hot lead mean to you?").
- **Ask for the inverse** ("ones that look hot but aren't").
- **Ask "what did you try?"** The failed fixes (Priya's keyword filter, Tanya's online waitlist) show what *won't* work.
- **Ask "what would you not want?"** and **"who approves?"**
- **End by asking for labelled examples**, which become your test set, and for introductions to the people in the follow-ups.
- **Don't decide anything on the call.** "I'm not going to decide anything today."
