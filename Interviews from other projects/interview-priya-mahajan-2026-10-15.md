# Builder discovery interview: Priya Mahajan

Sprint 1 · Harbor Line Bikes · simulated client


**Date:** Thursday, October 15, 2026. **Format:** Video call. **Duration:** about 45 minutes. **Participants:** Interviewer, Priya Mahajan (operations lead, Harbor Line Bikes).

**Interviewer:** [00:00] Thanks for making time, Priya. I know mornings are your busy stretch, so I appreciate you doing this at two. Before anything else, can you tell me a bit about your role at Harbor Line and how long you've been there?

**Priya:** Sure. I'm the operations lead, which is a fancy way of saying I do everything that isn't fixing bikes or standing at the counter. This is my fourth season. I started at the front desk, then Doug, he owns the place, asked me to take over scheduling and the inbox and the staff rota, and it sort of grew from there. In summer we're about nine people. Right now, mid-October, we're down to five, and after Remembrance Day it's more like four.

**Interviewer:** When you say the inbox, which inbox is that?

**Priya:** The hello address. hello@harborlinebikes.ca. It's on the website, it's on the booking confirmations, it's on the stickers on the handlebars. Well, the stickers have the phone number too, but people email anyway. Everything comes into that one inbox.

**Interviewer:** Who else can see it?

**Priya:** Doug has access, I have access. It shows up as a second inbox in my Gmail, Connor set that up. Connor's Doug's nephew, he does our computer stuff. Jake, our weekend lead in summer, he has it on his phone too, I think? I'm actually not sure if Connor ever took that off. He might still have it.

**Interviewer:** Okay. Tell me about the last time you sorted it. Yesterday, if you did it yesterday.

**Priya:** I did. So yesterday was a Wednesday. I get in at eight, put the coffee on, and I open hello first thing, because if I don't, the counter staff start asking me about bookings I haven't seen. Yesterday there were, I want to say, fifty-something waiting from overnight, and then they keep trickling in while I'm working.

**Interviewer:** Walk me through what you actually did with the first few.

**Priya:** First one yesterday was from PedalBook, that's our booking system. It sends a copy of every online booking and cancellation to hello. Those I mostly skim and archive, unless it's a group of more than six, because then bookings needs to plan the fleet. Then there was a guy asking whether the waterfront trail is open past the Seaport because he'd heard about construction. That's a question, I just answer that one myself. Then a couple asking to move their Saturday booking to Sunday. That's bookings, so I forward it to the bookings group. Then a concierge from one of the downtown hotels asking about a group rate. That goes to bookings, but I also copy Doug because he likes to handle the hotels himself. Then a charge dispute, somebody saying they were charged a late fee and they weren't late. That's billing. And it just goes like that.

**Interviewer:** How do you forward them? What does that look like on your screen?

**Priya:** I open it, read it, hit forward, type bookings or repairs or billing, it autocompletes, I sometimes add a line on top like "group of 8, needs the cargo bikes," and send. Then I archive the original in hello so I know I've done it. If I'm not sure, I star it. Yesterday I had six starred by the end.

**Interviewer:** What happens to the starred ones?

**Priya:** They sit until I get a minute. Or until somebody emails again, angrier. [laughs] That's honestly how a lot of things get bumped up.

**Interviewer:** Was there anything for repairs yesterday?

**Priya:** A few. One was a supplier telling Marcus his brake pads shipped, which shouldn't even come to hello, but it does. One was a guy saying the bike he returned Sunday was making a clicking noise, just so we know. And one was a woman at a hotel saying her seat kept sliding down. That one I forwarded and texted Marcus, because she was riding that afternoon. That's the kind I watch for.

**Interviewer:** How long did yesterday's sort take?

**Priya:** Yesterday was maybe an hour and ten. That's a good day. In August it's an hour and a half easily, sometimes two. Mondays are the worst because nothing gets sorted over the weekend. A Monday in August, I've had a hundred and forty in there.

**Interviewer:** [06:10] I want to make sure I have the numbers right. When we first spoke you said about sixty a day.

**Priya:** Yeah, sixty a day is what I usually say. That's a weekday in season. But if you count Saturday and Sunday it's more, because weekends are when people are actually riding. So maybe it's more like seventy a day across the week? Honestly I've never counted. Sixty is what it feels like on a normal Tuesday.

**Interviewer:** Do you have a sense of how those break down? You mentioned bookings, repairs, billing.

**Priya:** Most of it's bookings. Out of sixty, maybe thirty-five are bookings stuff. Changes, questions, group requests, the PedalBook copies. Billing is maybe eight to ten. Deposits, refunds, the late-fee arguments, the corporate accounts that want an invoice. Repairs is smaller, six or seven, and only a few of those are actually urgent. In August, two or three a day, maybe. Then the rest is junk, or not really any of the three. Suppliers, the tourism newsletter, job applications, review notifications, people trying to sell us search ads. Actually, now that I say it out loud, the junk might be more than I think. Maybe ten a day.

**Interviewer:** You said "not really any of the three." What kinds of things fall there?

**Priya:** Lost and found is a big one. "I left my sunglasses in the basket." Complaints that aren't about a charge. Damage claims are the worst, though.

**Interviewer:** Why the worst?

**Priya:** Because they're two things at once. Somebody writes, "the bike fell over in the wind at Point Pleasant, the derailleur's bent, are you going to charge me for this?" So the repair team needs to look at the bike, and billing needs to decide on the damage charge, and they need to talk to each other, and the customer is sometimes already home in Ontario. I forward those to both. And then both of them think the other one is handling it.

**Interviewer:** How often do those come in?

**Priya:** Damage claims? A few a week in summer. Three, four. Not every day.

**Interviewer:** Let's talk about the repair ones. You said only a few are urgent. What does urgent mean to you?

**Priya:** Urgent means someone is on a bike right now and something is wrong with it. Or they can't use it and they're paying for it. The classic is the battery dies halfway to the Dingle, or the motor cuts out on a hill. Brakes feeling soft, that's urgent because it's a safety thing, even if they say "it's probably fine." A flat tire out at the park. Lost the lock key and the bike is locked to a rack downtown. Those are urgent.

**Interviewer:** And the ones that sound urgent but aren't?

**Priya:** Oh, lots. The clicking-noise guy from yesterday. That's a repair for the bike, but it's not urgent for the customer, he's done riding. Or someone who writes in all caps, "YOUR BIKE RUINED MY DAY," and you read it and the bike broke down on Sunday and now they want money back. That's billing, really, and a complaint. It's loud, but it's not urgent anymore. The breakdown already happened.

**Interviewer:** And the reverse? Ones that don't look urgent?

**Priya:** That's the scary one. The cruise people. Cruise season here is September and the first half of October. The ships come in at the Seaport, passengers walk off and rent from us for the day, and they have to be back on board by four-thirty, five, whatever the ship says. A lot of them don't have phone plans that work in Canada, so they don't call the number on the sticker. They find wifi at a café and email. And they write these very polite emails. "Hello, so sorry to bother you, our bicycle seems to have stopped working, we are at the café near the lighthouse." Very calm. No "urgent" in the subject. Sometimes it's in German. If that sits for three hours, they miss their ship. That has never actually happened, knock on wood, but we came close.

**Interviewer:** Close how?

**Priya:** Doug drove out in his own truck once and then paid for a taxi for the couple back to the terminal. Last year, I think. You could ask him.

**Interviewer:** [12:20] I'd like to go to a specific one that didn't go well. Is there a recent urgent repair email you remember?

**Priya:** Yes. The Thanksgiving one. So Monday the 12th was Thanksgiving. We were open, but I wasn't in. Tuesday the 13th I come in, and it's a long-weekend backlog, so it's like a hundred and fifty emails, maybe more. I started at eight-fifteen. There was a woman who'd emailed at seven-forty that morning. They had a two-day rental, two bikes. She'd charged one of the batteries at their hotel overnight, and in the morning it wouldn't turn on, the display just showed an error, E-something. They had a harbour tour booked and wanted to know if they could swap the battery before ten.

**Interviewer:** What time did you get to it?

**Priya:** I looked it up after, because Doug asked me. I forwarded it to repairs at ten fifty-two.

**Interviewer:** So a bit over three hours after it came in.

**Priya:** Three hours and change. I was going oldest first, the way I always do. The subject line was something like "Question about battery." It just looked like a question.

**Interviewer:** What happened after ten fifty-two?

**Priya:** So this is the part that drives me a bit crazy. Marcus, he's our head mechanic, didn't see it until after lunch. He called her around one-twenty. By then she'd walked both bikes back to the shop, which is about twenty minutes from that hotel, pushing an e-bike, which is heavy. And she'd already talked to the counter and asked for her money back for both days.

**Interviewer:** You said Marcus didn't see it until after lunch. Can you say more about that?

**Priya:** Well. [pause] Marcus checks the repairs inbox on the tablet at the bench. But he's got his hands in a bike most of the day, so he checks it when he opens up, after lunch, and before he goes home. That's just how he works. In summer we have two more mechanics, so somebody's usually closer to the tablet. In October it's just Marcus, and on Saturdays we have a part-timer, Ellie.

**Interviewer:** So on the thirteenth, even once it was forwarded, it waited a couple more hours.

**Priya:** Yeah. I don't want to throw Marcus under the bus. If I'd flagged it, if I'd texted him, he'd have dropped everything. When I know it's urgent, I text him or I walk back to the shop. The problem is I didn't know it was urgent, because I hadn't read it yet. But, yeah. Honestly even when I get stuff to him fast, he doesn't always see it fast. That's true.

**Interviewer:** How often would you say that happens, something forwarded quickly but sitting in the repairs inbox?

**Priya:** I don't know. I've never tracked it. I'd be guessing. You'd have to ask Marcus, and he'll say never. [laughs]

**Interviewer:** How did the Thanksgiving one end?

**Priya:** We refunded both days. That was a hundred and eighty-nine plus tax for the one bike, and Doug comped the second bike too because she was upset, so call it three-eighty. And she left a two-star review on Google. It actually says "emailed at 7:40, nobody answered until after lunch." Doug has that one memorized.

**Interviewer:** Is that typical, the refund and the review?

**Priya:** The refund, in summer, yeah. I'd say two or three times a week in August we're giving someone something back because a repair was slow. Not always a full refund. Sometimes a free hour, a free day next time. The review, not usually. Most people don't bother.

**Interviewer:** Why do you think the urgent ones end up waiting? In your own words.

**Priya:** Because they all look the same when they come in. It's a list of names and subject lines, and the subject lines are useless. "Question." "Hi." "Booking." "Re: Your rental tomorrow." Nothing tells you this one is a woman standing outside a hotel with a dead battery. So you have to open every single one, and the only person who opens every single one is me, and I can only read one at a time. And then, okay, the Marcus part. It's a bit of both.

**Interviewer:** [18:30] Let me step back. Who else feels it when the inbox is slow? Not just the repair ones.

**Priya:** The counter staff, because customers walk in and say "I emailed you," and they've got nothing. Bookings, because they find out about a group of twelve the day before. Billing, which is Doug and our bookkeeper, Wendy, she's part-time, because the disputes get old and people go to their credit card company instead. And me, obviously. I spend the first hour and a half of my day reading other people's email.

**Interviewer:** What would you be doing with that time otherwise?

**Priya:** Staff schedules, which I do at night right now. The fleet plan for the cruise days. In winter I'm supposed to be getting the spring corporate bookings sorted, and I'm always behind on that. Doug wants me to do more with the hotels. There's a list.

**Interviewer:** How is the off-season different for the inbox?

**Priya:** Way quieter. Maybe twenty, twenty-five a day in winter. We do tune-ups on people's own bikes in winter, so "repairs" changes. It's more "can I book my bike in for a service in January," which isn't urgent at all. The rental breakdowns almost stop. We still rent in winter, a few hardy people and the fat-tire bikes, but once the last cruise ships have gone, from about the third week of October, I'd say we get maybe three urgent repair emails a week. Maybe fewer.

**Interviewer:** So from late October through the winter, the urgent ones are rare.

**Priya:** Rare. Which is honestly when I'd have time to try something new, but it's also when you wouldn't notice whether it worked. [laughs] Summer is when it matters.

**Interviewer:** When you're away, who sorts the inbox?

**Priya:** Doug. And it's a disaster. I took a week in July, my sister's wedding in Moncton, and Doug did it. He forwarded everything to everyone. Marcus got every booking change, bookings got the invoices. He said it took him three hours a day. Marcus told me he stopped opening the repairs inbox that week because it was all noise.

**Interviewer:** And weekends?

**Priya:** In theory, nobody sorts on weekends. Jake keeps an eye on it on his phone, and if he sees something about a broken bike he'll go tell whoever's in the shop. So I guess technically I'm not the only one. But nobody forwards anything on weekends. It just piles up for me on Monday.

**Interviewer:** [23:00] You mentioned before the call that you'd heard about tools that sort email automatically. What's your take on that?

**Priya:** Doug keeps sending me articles. "AI reads your inbox for you." And I get why, it would be nice. But I don't trust a machine to decide what's urgent. Not because I'm a dinosaur. I've actually tried.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about what you tried.

**Priya:** Two things. First, Gmail has those tabs, Primary, Promotions, whatever. Connor turned those on a couple of years ago to help. And a hotel concierge, a really good one, she sends us maybe twenty bookings a summer, her emails went into Promotions because they had the hotel logo and an unsubscribe footer from their system. I didn't see them for a week. She was not impressed. So I had Connor turn the tabs off.

**Interviewer:** And the second?

**Priya:** I made a filter. Anything with "broken" in it gets a red label. Seemed smart. Except PedalBook sends emails that say "If this link is broken, copy and paste the address below." So every booking copy turned red. And the real breakdown that week said "the bike died." Not broken. The filter caught everything except the thing I wanted. I deleted it after two days.

**Interviewer:** What did that teach you?

**Priya:** That people don't write the way you think. The polite cruise lady doesn't say urgent. The angry guy says URGENT about a late fee. Something that looks for words is going to get that wrong. I read for, I don't know, the situation. Is this person standing on a sidewalk right now?

**Interviewer:** What would you need to see to trust a change, any change, to how this works?

**Priya:** I'd need to see why. If something gets put somewhere, I want to know why it went there. And I want to be able to check it. At least at first, I'd want to look at everything for a couple of weeks and see if I agree. And the urgent ones, if one gets missed, I need to know it was missed. Not find out from a Google review.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything you'd definitely not want?

**Priya:** Don't email the customers back automatically. I've seen those "We've received your message and will respond within 48 hours" emails, and if I was stuck on a bike and got that, I'd lose my mind. Don't delete anything. Don't move things somewhere I can't see them, I want hello to stay the place where everything is. And please don't make me log into another thing. I have PedalBook, Gmail, the bank, the card terminal portal, the scheduling app. I'm done.

**Interviewer:** That's clear. Anything about the customers' information?

**Priya:** Oh, yeah. People send us everything. Phone numbers, which hotel and room they're in. Some people send a photo of their driver's licence because the waiver asks for ID and they don't want to stand in line. Every couple of weeks somebody types their whole credit card number into an email to pay a damage charge. I delete those and ask them to call. And photos. Damage photos, and once a photo of a guy's knee after he fell, which I did not need to see. Doug is very particular about where that stuff goes. You'd have to ask him, but he won't want it ending up somewhere random.

**Interviewer:** [29:40] Let's talk about the systems for a minute. You said Connor handles the computer side. What does he look after?

**Priya:** Our Google account, the whole thing. He set up the groups, bookings, repairs, billing. Those are groups that send to the right people. He set up hello so Doug and I can both get into it. After someone clicked a fake invoice last year, he locked things down, so now if you try to connect some app to your Gmail it says you need admin approval, and that's Connor. He lives in Moncton and does this on the side, so he's not instant. Sometimes it's a week.

**Interviewer:** Do the staff have their own Google accounts?

**Priya:** The full-time people, yes, and they have the code thing on their phones, the two-step. The front desk has a shared login, frontdesk@, that the summer staff all use. I'm not sure that one has the two-step. Probably not, because it's shared. Connor's been meaning to fix that.

**Interviewer:** And the repair team, besides that inbox on the tablet, how do they get work?

**Priya:** There's a whiteboard in the shop. Every bike that's in for repair gets a line. The counter writes on it, Marcus writes on it. If it's urgent and someone's in the building, they just walk back and tell him. Phone calls from customers on the road go to the front desk cell, and the front desk walks back or texts Marcus. Those get handled fast, honestly. Ten, fifteen minutes. It's the emails that sit.

**Interviewer:** Do you have any record of breakdowns? How many happen, how they were reported?

**Priya:** There's a damage log in a Google Sheet. That's mostly for billing, damage charges. It doesn't say how someone reported it. The repair stuff is on the whiteboard, and that gets wiped. So, no, not really. I could probably go back through the repairs group and count, if that would help.

**Interviewer:** It might. We'll come back to that. You said PedalBook copies bookings to hello. Does it do anything else with email?

**Priya:** It sends customers their confirmation and a reminder the day before. Customers sometimes reply to those reminders, and the reply comes to hello. So I get "Can we come at eleven instead?" with the whole booking underneath it. Those are easy, at least they've got the booking number. Although, actually, the Thanksgiving woman, I think hers was a reply to a reminder too. Which is maybe why it looked like a booking question.

**Interviewer:** [34:50] If you imagine this working the way you'd like, a few weeks from now, what's different about your morning?

**Priya:** I come in, and the stuff that's on fire has already been dealt with, or at least someone who can deal with it knows. And I'm not spending ninety minutes reading. I'd still want to look at the weird ones, the damage claims, the hotels. I like doing the hotels. But if I could get through the whole thing in fifteen minutes and just handle the odd ones, I'd be thrilled.

**Interviewer:** And for the customer with the dead battery at seven-forty?

**Priya:** Somebody on the repair side knows within the hour. Ideally less, but an hour, she'd have made her tour. Even if all Marcus can do is call her and say "walk it to the Seaport, we'll meet you with a battery."

**Interviewer:** You said "somebody on the repair side." Is that always Marcus?

**Priya:** In October, yes. In summer it'd be whoever's on. And if Marcus is out on a delivery, which happens, he drives bikes to hotels sometimes, then nobody's at the bench. Then it's the counter, I guess. I don't actually know who it should be in that case. That's a Doug question.

**Interviewer:** How would you know things were better? What would you look at?

**Priya:** Fewer "I emailed you" at the counter. Fewer refunds. Marcus not hearing about breakdowns from the customer walking in the door. And me not being behind on everything else. I don't know how you'd measure most of that. We don't track any of it.

**Interviewer:** Earlier you said ninety minutes a day. Yesterday was an hour ten, and Mondays in August are over two hours. If you had to give one number for a typical week in season, what would it be?

**Priya:** Seven, seven and a half hours a week. That's fair. Less in October. Way less in winter. Mondays drag the average up.

**Interviewer:** Of that time, how much is deciding where things go, versus answering things yourself?

**Priya:** Huh. I never split it. I answer maybe eight to ten myself a day, the quick ones, trail questions, opening hours. Those take a couple of minutes each. So maybe twenty minutes of the ninety is me writing replies? The rest is reading and forwarding and going back to the starred ones.

**Interviewer:** [39:30] A couple more questions. Do repair emails ever come from someone other than the customer?

**Priya:** Hotels, sometimes. A concierge will email "your guest in room 412 says the bike won't start." Which is great because I know the hotel, but it's someone else's problem being reported secondhand, and I can't always reach the guest. And Marcus's parts suppliers, like I said. Those go to repairs too, but they're not urgent at all.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything about how the other teams use their groups that I should know?

**Priya:** Bookings is three people in summer, two now. They're good. Billing is Doug and Wendy. Wendy works Tuesdays and Thursdays, so billing stuff that comes in on a Friday sits until Tuesday unless Doug grabs it, and Doug doesn't. But billing's not urgent. Mostly.

**Interviewer:** Mostly?

**Priya:** Damage deposits. If somebody's deposit hold is about to drop off their card, there's a window. But that's in the weeds. Wendy would know better.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything I haven't asked about that you think matters?

**Priya:** Just that I don't want this to turn into a thing where it looks like I'm slow. I'm not slow. It's sixty emails. Some days it's eighty. And honestly, if Marcus looked at the tablet more, half of this would go away. But I also know if I'd read that battery email at eight-twenty instead of ten-fifty, it would have been fine. So it's both.

**Interviewer:** That's fair, and I'll keep both in mind. I'm not going to decide anything today. I'd like to talk to Doug tomorrow about the money side and the privacy side. And if you're okay with it, I'd like a rough count of the last two weeks of the repairs group: how many came in, and roughly when someone on the repair side responded, if that's visible.

**Priya:** I can do that. It'll be rough. Some of his responses are just him walking out front.

**Interviewer:** Rough is fine. I'd also like ten minutes with Marcus, if he's willing, and some time with Connor on how the accounts are set up. Would you introduce me?

**Priya:** Sure. Marcus will be easier to get than Connor. Connor answers email at midnight.

**Interviewer:** One last thing. Could you send me three or four of the trickier emails from this month with the personal details blacked out? A cruise one, a damage claim, the Thanksgiving one.

**Priya:** I'll black out the names and the hotel stuff. Give me till Monday.
